Renting an Apartment in China as a Foreigner

Most guides tell you which app to use. That is not what loses people the apartment. These four things are.

You find a listing, you message the landlord, and then one of four things kills the deal — and none of them is the price you saw online. This is what to settle before you travel to view.

01The 24-hour registration rule

Every foreigner in China must register their residential address with the local police within 24 hours of moving in. Hotels do it for you. A private rental does not.

  • If you rent privately, you do it yourself — at the local police station, or through the exit-entry mini-program in WeChat where available.
  • You need the landlord's cooperation: their ID, the property certificate, and a signed lease. Many landlords do not want this paperwork, and that is exactly why they refuse foreign tenants.
  • Keep the registration slip. It is checked at visa renewal and can affect it.

Ask before you view: "Will you register a foreigner at this address?" If the answer is vague, the apartment is not available to you no matter what the listing says.

02押一付三: the cash you need on signing day

The standard private-lease structure is 押一付三 — one month deposit plus three months rent, paid on the day you sign.

On a 5,000 RMB apartment that is 20,000 RMB before you move a single box in. Foreign cards mostly work for daily spending but not reliably for a landlord's deposit, so plan how that money arrives before you go viewing.

03The agency fee is negotiable — most people never ask

The usual agency fee is half to one month's rent, and in a soft market it is routinely split with the landlord.

  • Zero-fee direct-rent platforms exist and are worth checking first.
  • If you sign the same day, you have leverage. Say so, plainly, before you view.
  • Agents may not charge you a viewing fee. If someone asks for one, walk.

04Paying without a Chinese bank card

Alipay and WeChat Pay both accept foreign Visa and Mastercard now, so daily life works without a Chinese bank account. Two gaps remain:

  • Some landlords want rent into a Chinese bank account, or cash.
  • Setting up a local bank account needs your passport, visa and often a phone number and address registration — which loops back to section 01.

05What usually goes wrong

  • Commercial utilities. 商水商电 costs several times 民水民电. Confirm which the building uses.
  • Illegal partitions. Subdivided flats are cheap and can be shut down.
  • Photos that are not the flat. Ask for a live video walkthrough with the door number visible, not a photo set.
  • Deposit terms. Get the refund condition in writing in the contract.
